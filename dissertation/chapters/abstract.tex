% Abstract

This dissertation examines the ideal communication range for enhancing collective yield in multi-agent resource collection settings. We use an agent-based simulation framework built on the Mesa platform to see how different communication ranges ($r = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8$) affect the performance of autonomous harvesting agents working in a $30 \times 30$ grid with fruit resources distributed randomly.

Our experimental results, derived from 100 independent simulation runs for each configuration, indicate that local communication (r = 2) achieves the highest efficiency, with a normalized efficiency score of 912.5 units per message exchanged. All communication-enabled configurations ($r > 0$) show small improvements in mean yield compared to baseline ($r = 0$: $1682.7 \pm 514.6$). However, the gains are small (3.0\% at $r = 2$, 1.9\% at $r = 4$, and 0.1\% at $r = 8$) and not statistically significant ($p = 0.891$). It's important to note that communication overhead goes up a lot with range, going from 1.9 messages at $r = 2$ to 16.0 messages at $r = 8$. At the same time, efficiency drops a lot, from 912.5 to 102.3.

The descriptive analysis shows that environmental factors, especially the number of resources ($p < 0.001$) and the size of the team ($p < 0.001$), have a much bigger impact on collective yield than communication range. It turns out that exploration is more important than coordination when it comes to how well harvesting works in big, empty spaces. A model of interference for ranges $r > 4$ shows that too much communication makes it harder to coordinate things without really helping.

This study helps us learn more about the trade-offs between communication and coordination in distributed multi-agent systems. It also has real-world implications for making swarm robotics applications that work well in precision farming, warehouse automation, and search-and-rescue missions. A Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) agent architecture and selective broadcasting protocols are used in the study to model realistic autonomous behavior. At \href{https://github.com/marufdevops/multiagent-cooperation-research}{GitHub Repository} you can find the full implementation, experimental data, and analysis scripts.

\textbf{Keywords:} Multi-agent systems, communication range optimization, swarm intelligence, agent-based modeling, resource gathering, BDI architecture, Mesa framework
