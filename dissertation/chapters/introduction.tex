\section{Background and Motivation}

The coordination of multiple autonomous agents for resource gathering tasks is an important problem in robotics and artificial intelligence. In agricultural settings, cooperative robot teams for fruit harvesting could help address labor shortages and improve efficiency. However, effective coordination requires agents to communicate information about resource locations. The design of communication protocols—particularly the selection of appropriate communication ranges—impacts the performance of multi-agent systems.

Communication in multi-agent systems involves trade-offs. Too little communication leads to isolated agents without access to information from teammates. Too much communication introduces overhead costs including bandwidth consumption and message processing. The optimal communication range likely depends on factors such as team size, resource distribution, and environmental dynamics.

This dissertation explores communication range effects in multi-agent fruit harvesting through simulation-based investigation. We focus on understanding how communication range affects collective yield in different environmental conditions.

\section{Research Questions}

This dissertation investigates the following research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} How does communication range affect collective yield in a grid-based multi-agent fruit harvesting environment?

    \item \textbf{RQ2:} How do resource dynamics (Static vs. Replenishing) influence the relationship between communication range and collective performance?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Objectives}

The objectives of this research are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To implement a basic agent-based simulation of multi-agent fruit harvesting using the Mesa 3.0 framework, incorporating agent behaviors for movement, harvesting, and communication.

    \item To develop an interactive visualization system that allows real-time observation of agent behaviors and communication patterns.

    \item To conduct a literature review examining existing research on multi-agent coordination, communication protocols, and agricultural robotics.

    \item To establish a foundation for future experimental work exploring communication range effects across different environmental conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Contributions}

This early-stage work (Weeks 1-2) establishes the foundation for investigating communication range effects in multi-agent fruit harvesting:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Working simulation:} A functional Mesa 3.0 implementation with agent behaviors, communication protocols, and two resource dynamics modes.

    \item \textbf{Interactive visualization:} A web-based interface for observing agent behaviors and adjusting simulation parameters in real-time.

    \item \textbf{Literature foundation:} A review of relevant research on multi-agent coordination and communication protocols.

    \item \textbf{Future research direction:} A clear path toward systematic experimentation and statistical analysis of communication range effects.
\end{itemize}
